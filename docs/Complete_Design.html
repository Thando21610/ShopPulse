Part A

The Problem & The Solution

1.Executive Summary

ShopPulse is a lightweight retail operations and inventory intelligence platform for small South African spaza shops. The proposed pilot establishment is Thandabantu Spaza Shop, a small, one-operator retail business.

Initial informal observation suggests three connected operational pressures: customer congestion during busy periods when a single person handles serving, payment and stock; perishable stock — especially bread — that can expire before it sells; and the absence of a structured daily management process for sales, stock and cash. These observations are the starting hypothesis, not confirmed findings. They will be validated in a structured owner interview before development priorities are locked.

The central design idea: one completed sale should update everything that depends on it — transaction record, stock levels, batch allocation, expiry exposure, daily financial summaries and the audit trail — without repeated manual administration.

Product vision

To make small retail businesses easier to run by giving owners and operators a simple, affordable and intelligent way to manage sales, stock, expiry dates and daily financial activity.

Tagline

ShopPulse — Smarter operations. Less waste. Better business.

Design principle A small tool one shop owner uses every day beats an impressive application nobody needs. Scope is deliberately constrained to what the owner confirms is useful. Polish and reliability are prioritised over technical complexity.

4.1 Unique value proposition

A single sale, committed once, automatically:

  1. Records the sale and its line items with historical price snapshots.
  2. Calculates the total and change in ZAR on the server.
  3. Records the payment method and amounts.
  4. Deducts stock from the correct batch using FEFO.
  5. Writes stock movement ledger entries.
  6. Updates reporting projections.
  7. Records the responsible user.
  8. Writes an audit event.

4.2 Deliberately out of scope

  • Full accounting, VAT submissions or payroll.
  • Customer accounts, loyalty and marketing.
  • Multi-branch franchise management.
  • Microservices, message brokers and Kubernetes.
  • AI/ML forecasting — replaced by transparent rules-based recommendations.

Use Cases & Processes

7.Core Use Cases

UC-05 · Process a sale (primary flow)

Actors: Operator. Goal: complete a purchase while keeping stock, cash and records consistent.

  1. Operator opens New Sale (optionally with Rush Mode on).
  2. Operator searches products by name or SKU and adds them to the cart.
  3. Backend retrieves current prices and checks stock availability.
  4. Backend calculates subtotal, any authorised discount, and the grand total in ZAR.
  5. Operator selects the payment method and, for cash, enters the amount tendered.
  6. Backend calculates change.
  7. Operator confirms. Backend opens a database transaction and:
  1. Creates the sale and its line items (with price snapshots).
  2. Creates the payment record.
  3. Deducts stock from the correct batches using FEFO.
  4. Writes stock movement ledger entries.
  5. Writes the audit event.
  6. Commits.

Only after commit does the UI display the successful receipt.

Alternative paths

ConditionSystem behaviour
Product out of stockSale blocked; clear message; no partial record
Product deactivatedCannot be added to cart
Price changed before confirmationBackend recalculates; operator sees updated total before confirming
Cash tendered insufficientSale blocked; change not calculated
Duplicate submission (double tap / retry)Idempotency key prevents a second sale
Two operators sell the last unitConcurrency control ensures only one succeeds
Database transaction failsFull rollback; no sale, no stock deduction

UC-03 · Receive stock

Actors: Owner / authorised operator. Creates a new stock batch and updates inventory.

  1. Operator selects product and enters quantity, unit cost (ZAR), supplier (optional), batch reference and expiry date (where relevant).
  2. Backend validates quantity, cost, date and authorisation.
  3. Batch is created, stock movement recorded, inventory updated, audit event written — all in one transaction.

Failure rule: a stock receipt is never partially saved.

UC-07 · Record expired or damaged stock

  1. Select product and batch; enter quantity and reason (Expired / Damaged / Missing / Spoiled / Other).
  2. Backend checks the batch has enough remaining quantity.
  3. Waste record and stock movement are written; audit event created.
  4. Inventory and the waste report update.

UC-09 · Open and close a cash shift

Opening: operator enters the opening float; the shift becomes active.

Closing: the system calculates expected cash, the operator counts physical cash and enters the actual amount, the system calculates the variance, and the operator explains any difference.

Expected Cash = Opening Float + Cash Sales − Cash Refunds + Cash In − Cash Out
Variance      = Actual Cash − Expected Cash

UC-08 · Monitor expiry dates

The Expiry Dashboard retrieves batches with expiry dates, calculates days remaining and groups them as Expired, Expiring Soon or Safe. It recommends FEFO rotation but never silently deletes or adjusts stock — a human records waste.

9.Business Rules

  1. All monetary values are in ZAR and stored as decimals with two decimal places.
  2. Prices and totals are always validated and calculated on the server.
  3. Completed sales cannot be deleted; corrections are compensating refunds.
  4. Refunds must reference the original sale.
  5. Stock movements are traceable — every quantity change has a ledger entry.
  6. Stock cannot become negative unless an explicitly approved policy permits it.
  7. Expired stock is flagged, never auto-deleted.
  8. FEFO (first-expiry-first-out) is used for perishables; FIFO where no expiry exists.
  9. Every important action generates an audit event.
  10. Every tenant-owned record must belong to the authenticated business.
  11. Permissions are enforced by the backend, not the client.
  12. No real customer personal data appears anywhere in the demonstration.
  13. The application must remain usable on a mid-range Android phone.

13.3 Normalisation

  • 1NF: no comma-separated multi-value fields; permissions use a junction table.
  • 2NF: non-key attributes depend on the whole key; role assignments live in their own table.
  • 3NF: supplier details stored once, not repeated on every batch.
  • Denormalisation: read projections (StockBalance, DailySalesSummary) may be added for dashboards, but the movement ledger and sale records remain authoritative.

23.Same-Origin Proxy Decision

This is the single most consequential wiring decision in the document.

23.1 The problem

Cloudflare Pages and Cloud Run are different sites. A cookie set by the API is therefore a third-party cookie when read from the frontend origin. Safari's ITP and Chrome's ongoing third-party cookie restrictions make that unreliable for a graded demonstration.

23.2 Options compared

23.3 Proxy implementation

// functions/api/[[path]].js  — Cloudflare Pages Function
export async function onRequest(context) {
  const { request, env, params } = context;
  const target = env.API_ORIGIN;              // e.g. https://shoppulse-api-xxxx.run.app
  const path   = (params.path || []).join("/");
  const url    = new URL(request.url);

  const upstream = new URL(`${target}/api/${path}`);
  upstream.search = url.search;

  const headers = new Headers(request.headers);
  headers.set("X-Forwarded-Host", url.host);
  headers.delete("host");

  const init = {
    method: request.method,
    headers,
    redirect: "manual",
    body: ["GET", "HEAD"].includes(request.method) ? undefined : request.body,
  };

  const res = await fetch(upstream.toString(), init);

  const out = new Headers(res.headers);
  out.delete("content-encoding");   // fetch has already decoded the body
  out.delete("content-length");

  return new Response(res.body, { status: res.status, headers: out });
}
Cookie attributes still matter Because the proxy makes everything first-party, the API should set the refresh cookie with Domain omitted (host-only), Path=/api/auth, SameSite=Strict, Secure and HttpOnly. The cookie is then only transmitted on the two endpoints that need it.

25.Refresh Rotation & Reuse Detection

Each refresh consumes the presented token and issues a replacement. A token is therefore single-use.

25.1 The mechanism

  1. Client presents refresh token R1.
  2. Server validates R1: exists, not revoked, not expired.
  3. Server issues R2 in the same family and marks R1 as revoked with ReplacedByTokenId = R2.
  4. Server returns a new access token and the new R2 cookie.
  5. If R1 is ever presented again, the server knows it was already used — a signal that it was captured.

25.2 Family model

Login ──► R1 ──rotate──► R2 ──rotate──► R3 ──rotate──► R4
          │              │              │              │

                    all share FamilyId = F

Reuse of R1 detected

        ▼

        │
        ▼
All future refreshes with any R in F return 401 AUTH_REFRESH_REUSED

25.5 Rotation service

public sealed class RefreshTokenService
{
    private readonly AppDbContext _db;
    private readonly RefreshTokenOptions _opt;

    public (string Raw, RefreshToken Entity) Rotate(RefreshToken current)
    {
        var now = DateTimeOffset.UtcNow;
        var raw = GenerateRaw();                    // 32 random bytes, base64url

        var next = new RefreshToken
        {
            Id           = Guid.NewGuid(),
            UserId       = current.UserId,
            FamilyId     = current.FamilyId,
            TokenHash    = Hash(raw),
            CreatedAtUtc = now,
            // Idle window restarts, but never beyond the family's absolute cap.
            ExpiresAtUtc = Min(now.AddDays(_opt.IdleDays), current.FamilyAbsoluteExpiry),
        };

        current.RevokedAtUtc      = now;
        current.RevokedReason     = "ROTATED";
        current.ReplacedByTokenId = next.Id;

        return (raw, next);
    }

    public async Task RevokeFamilyAsync(Guid familyId, string reason, CancellationToken ct)
    {
        await _db.RefreshTokens
            .Where(t => t.FamilyId == familyId && t.RevokedAtUtc == null)
            .ExecuteUpdateAsync(s => s
                .SetProperty(t => t.RevokedAtUtc, DateTimeOffset.UtcNow)
                .SetProperty(t => t.RevokedReason, reason), ct);
    }

    private static string GenerateRaw()
        => Base64UrlEncode(RandomNumberGenerator.GetBytes(32));

    public static string Hash(string raw)
        => Convert.ToBase64String(SHA256.HashData(Encoding.UTF8.GetBytes(raw)));
}

27.2 The handler

public sealed class AuthMessageHandler : DelegatingHandler
{
    private static readonly SemaphoreSlim Gate = new(1, 1);

    private readonly ITokenStore _tokens;
    private readonly IHttpClientFactory _factory;
    private readonly NavigationManager _nav;
    private readonly IToastService _toasts;
    private readonly ILogger<AuthMessageHandler> _log;

    private static readonly TimeSpan RefreshWindow = TimeSpan.FromSeconds(60);

    protected override async Task<HttpResponseMessage> SendAsync(
        HttpRequestMessage request, CancellationToken ct)
    {
        // ---- 1. Proactive refresh if the token is about to expire -------------
        if (_tokens.IsExpiringWithin(RefreshWindow))
            await RefreshOnceAsync(ct);

        // ---- 2. Attach the bearer token ---------------------------------------
        AttachToken(request);

        // ---- 3. Buffer the body so the request can be replayed ----------------
        var buffered = await BufferIfNeededAsync(request, ct);

        // ---- 4. First attempt --------------------------------------------------
        var response = await base.SendAsync(request, ct);

        if (response.StatusCode != HttpStatusCode.Unauthorized)
            return response;

        // ---- 5. Reactive refresh + one retry ----------------------------------
        _log.LogInformation("401 received; attempting refresh before retry.");

        var refreshed = await RefreshOnceAsync(ct);
        if (!refreshed)
        {
            _tokens.Clear();
            _toasts.Warning("Your session ended. Please sign in again.");
            _nav.NavigateTo("/login?reason=session-expired");
            return response;
        }

        using var retry = await CloneAsync(request, buffered, ct);
        AttachToken(retry);

        return await base.SendAsync(retry, ct);
    }

    private void AttachToken(HttpRequestMessage request)
    {
        var token = _tokens.Current;
        if (token is null) return;
        request.Headers.Authorization = new AuthenticationHeaderValue("Bearer", token.Value);
    }

    private async Task<bool> RefreshOnceAsync(CancellationToken ct)
    {
        await Gate.WaitAsync(ct);
        try
        {
            // Another request may have refreshed while we waited.
            if (!_tokens.IsExpiringWithin(RefreshWindow))
                return true;

            var client = _factory.CreateClient("auth");   // handler-free client
            using var res = await client.PostAsync("api/auth/refresh", content: null, ct);

            if (!res.IsSuccessStatusCode) return false;

            var body = await res.Content.ReadFromJsonAsync<RefreshResponse>(cancellationToken: ct);
            if (body is null) return false;

            _tokens.Set(new AccessTokenSnapshot(body.AccessToken, body.ExpiresAtUtc));
            return true;
        }
        catch (Exception ex) when (ex is HttpRequestException or TaskCanceledException)
        {
            _log.LogWarning(ex, "Refresh call failed.");
            return false;
        }
        finally
        {
            Gate.Release();
        }
    }

    private static async Task<byte[]?> BufferIfNeededAsync(
        HttpRequestMessage request, CancellationToken ct)
    {
        if (request.Content is null) return null;
        return await request.Content.ReadAsByteArrayAsync(ct);
    }

    private static async Task<HttpRequestMessage> CloneAsync(
        HttpRequestMessage original, byte[]? body, CancellationToken ct)
    {
        var clone = new HttpRequestMessage(original.Method, original.RequestUri);

        foreach (var header in original.Headers)
            clone.Headers.TryAddWithoutValidation(header.Key, header.Value);

        if (body is not null)
        {
            clone.Content = new ByteArrayContent(body);
            foreach (var header in original.Content!.Headers)
                clone.Content.Headers.TryAddWithoutValidation(header.Key, header.Value);
        }

        await Task.CompletedTask;
        return clone;
    }
}
Why the body must be buffered An HttpRequestMessage cannot be sent twice — its content stream is already consumed. Retrying a POST therefore requires re-creating the request with a fresh content object. The buffered byte array makes that possible and keeps idempotency keys intact across the retry.
Never refresh on 403 The handler only reacts to 401. A 403 is a definitive authorisation decision and refreshing would produce the same result — burning a rotation for nothing and risking a false reuse detection.

28.Proactive vs Reactive Refresh

StrategyWhen it runsCostBenefit
Proactive Before sending, if exp - now < 60 s One extra round trip, off the critical path The user never sees a 401, never sees a retry delay
Reactive After receiving a 401 The user's request pays for a wasted round trip Handles server-side revocation, clock drift, tab-wake-from-sleep

Both are used. Proactive refresh handles the ordinary case cheaply; reactive refresh is the safety net.

                if (res.StatusCode == HttpStatusCode.NoContent)
                    return Result<T>.Ok(default!);

                var data = await res.Content.ReadFromJsonAsync<T>(cancellationToken: ct);
                return Result<T>.Ok(data!);
            }

            return Result<T>.Fail(await ReadProblemAsync(res, ct));
        }
        catch (TaskCanceledException) when (!ct.IsCancellationRequested)
        {
            return Result<T>.Fail(ApiError.Network());
        }
        catch (HttpRequestException)
        {
            return Result<T>.Fail(ApiError.Network());
        }
    }

    private static async Task<ApiError> ReadProblemAsync(
        HttpResponseMessage res, CancellationToken ct)
    {
        try
        {
            var problem = await res.Content.ReadFromJsonAsync<ProblemDetailsPayload>(cancellationToken: ct);
            if (problem is not null)
                return new ApiError(
                    problem.Code ?? "UNKNOWN",
                    problem.Detail ?? problem.Title ?? "Something went wrong.",
                    (int)res.StatusCode,
                    problem.Errors,
                    problem.CorrelationId);
        }
        catch (JsonException) { /* fall through */ }

        return new ApiError("UNKNOWN", "Something went wrong. Please try again.",
            (int)res.StatusCode, null, null);
    }
}

35.2 Mapping server errors onto Blazor fields

public static class ValidationMapper
{
    public static void ApplyTo(
        EditContext editContext,
        IReadOnlyDictionary<string, string[]> fieldErrors)
    {
        var messageStore = new ValidationMessageStore(editContext);

        foreach (var (fieldName, messages) in fieldErrors)
        {
            var identifier = editContext
                .FieldIdentifiers()
                .FirstOrDefault(f =>
                    string.Equals(f.FieldName, fieldName, StringComparison.OrdinalIgnoreCase));

            if (identifier == default)
            {
                // Unmapped error -> surface as a form-level banner
                messageStore.Add(new FieldIdentifier(editContext.Model, string.Empty),
                    messages.First());
                continue;
            }

            foreach (var message in messages)
                messageStore.Add(identifier, message);
        }

        editContext.NotifyValidationStateChanged();
    }
}

37.Toast System

37.1 Behaviour

  • Fixed host at top-right on desktop, bottom-center on mobile.
  • Maximum of 4 visible; oldest evicted on a fifth.
  • Identical messages within 2 seconds are deduplicated.
  • Success / info auto-dismiss; errors auto-dismiss but remain manually closable.
  • Hovering pauses the dismiss timer.
  • aria-live="polite"; errors use role="alert".

37.2 Service

public enum ToastKind { Success, Info, Warning, Error }

public sealed record Toast(Guid Id, ToastKind Kind, string Message, string? Detail, int DurationMs);

public interface IToastService
{
    event Action? Changed;
    IReadOnlyList<Toast> Visible { get; }

    void Success(string message, string? detail = null);
    void Info(string message, string? detail = null);
    void Warning(string message, string? detail = null);
    void Error(string message, string? detail = null);
    void Dismiss(Guid id);
}

public sealed class ToastService : IToastService
{
    private readonly List<Toast> _items = new();
    private readonly Dictionary<string, DateTimeOffset> _recent = new();

    public event Action? Changed;
    public IReadOnlyList<Toast> Visible => _items;

    public void Success(string message, string? detail = null)
        => Push(ToastKind.Success, message, detail, 4_000);

    public void Info(string message, string? detail = null)
        => Push(ToastKind.Info, message, detail, 4_000);

    public void Warning(string message, string? detail = null)
        => Push(ToastKind.Warning, message, detail, 6_000);

    public void Error(string message, string? detail = null)
        => Push(ToastKind.Error, message, detail, 8_000);

    private void Push(ToastKind kind, string message, string? detail, int durationMs)
    {
        var key = $"{kind}|{message}";
        var now = DateTimeOffset.UtcNow;

        if (_recent.TryGetValue(key, out var last) && now - last < TimeSpan.FromSeconds(2))
            return;

        _recent[key] = now;
        if (_items.Count >= 4) _items.RemoveAt(0);
        _items.Add(new Toast(Guid.NewGuid(), kind, message, detail, durationMs));
        Changed?.Invoke();
    }

    public void Dismiss(Guid id)
    {
        _items.RemoveAll(t => t.Id == id);
        Changed?.Invoke();
    }
}

41.Concurrency & Optimistic Locking

The scenario that matters: two operators sell the last loaf of bread at the same moment.

41.1 Mechanism

Every row that holds mutable quantity carries a concurrency token. EF Core includes it in the WHERE clause of the UPDATE. If zero rows are affected, the write is rejected.

{

41.2 Handling the conflict

try
{
    await _db.SaveChangesAsync(ct);
}
catch (DbUpdateConcurrencyException)
{
    return Problem(
        statusCode: 409,
        title: "Stock changed",
        detail: "Someone else sold this item first. Please refresh and try again.",
        extensions: new() { ["code"] = "CONCURRENCY_CONFLICT" });
}

41.3 UI behaviour on conflict

  1. Banner appears above the cart: "Stock changed while you were serving. Refresh to see current quantities."
  2. The affected line is highlighted in red.
  3. A Refresh button reloads the cart lines from the server.
  4. The operator adjusts and resubmits with a new idempotency key, because the intent has changed.
PostgreSQL detail PostgreSQL has no native rowversion. EF Core maps [Timestamp] onto the system column xmin when configured with UseXminAsConcurrencyToken(), or onto an explicit integer column incremented by a trigger. Either approach yields the same optimistic-lock behaviour.

43.Stock Rescue

43.1 The problem it solves

Traditional stock tracking tells an owner how many products remain. It does not tell them whether those products will sell before they expire. Twenty loaves of bread on the shelf is good news at 07:00 and bad news at 17:00.

43.2 The concept

ShopPulse combines four inputs:

  1. Current stock quantity per batch
  2. Expiry dates and days remaining
  3. Recent sales history per product
  4. Time-of-day demand patterns

It then flags batches that are at risk of expiring before they sell, and recommends an action: prioritise the oldest batch, discount it, or reduce the next order quantity.

43.3 Worked example (illustrative test data)

Honesty over hype With a small dataset, this is a transparent rules-based recommendation — not AI forecasting. The UI will show the reasoning and the data behind each suggestion. Demand-pattern learning is a later phase, and will always display its assumptions and limitations.

43.4 Later: demand-aware recommendations

Once enough reliable sales history exists, the system can estimate average daily sales, morning versus afternoon demand and weekly patterns, then recommend order quantities. This remains an enhancement, not a dependency of the core solution.

55.Acceptance Criteria

Authentication

  • Logging in returns an access token and sets an HttpOnly refresh cookie with the correct flags
  • The access token does not appear in localStorage, sessionStorage or any cookie
  • Refreshing the page keeps the user signed in via silent refresh on boot
  • Refresh rotates: a second call shows a different cookie value
  • Replaying an old refresh token returns AUTH_REFRESH_REUSED and forces re-login
  • Logout clears the cookie and the old token no longer refreshes

Request pipeline

  • An expired access token produces exactly one refresh call, then a successful retry
  • Three simultaneous 401s produce exactly one /api/auth/refresh request
  • A 403 never triggers a refresh
  • A cross-tenant request returns 404, not 200

Validation & feedback

  • Submitting an invalid form shows centered red text under each offending field with a ⚠ prefix
  • Invalid inputs carry aria-invalid="true" and a red border
  • Validation errors are never delivered as toasts
  • Successful writes show a success toast; failed writes show either inline errors or a banner — never both for the same message
  • Every toast is readable on a 360 px wide screen

Data integrity

  • Submitting the same sale twice with the same idempotency key creates one sale
  • Two concurrent attempts on the last unit produce one success and one CONCURRENCY_CONFLICT
  • No completed sale is ever deleted; corrections are refunds
  • Every write that moves stock or money produces an audit event in the same transaction

Resilience

  • A simulated cold start shows the info banner and the request still completes
  • Going offline mid-form disables submit and shows the offline banner
  • A timed-out sale retry produces exactly one sale
  • A 500 shows the correlation ID so the failure can be traced in logs

Visual assets

  • No hero images, no split-card layouts on forms
  • All icons render from the self-hosted Material Symbols subset
  • Product thumbnails are coloured initials, not uploaded images
  • Performance budget holds: framework ≤ 1 MB Brotli, total app payload ≤ 1.4 MB
  • First meaningful paint on throttled 3G is under 3 seconds

56.Conclusion

ShopPulse is a deliberately small, practical solution to a specific operational problem: a one-person spaza shop that must serve customers, manage stock and account for cash at the same time.

Its central idea is integration over accumulation. One committed sale updates the transaction record, stock levels, batch allocation, expiry exposure, daily financials and the audit trail — so the operator does not repeat work the system can do once.

The architecture uses Clean Architecture, SOLID principles and ACID transactions to keep that promise reliable. Financial operations use ZAR with decimal precision and server-side validation. Security is enforced on the backend, with tenant isolation and a POPIA-conscious approach that avoids collecting customer personal data entirely.

The authentication design is deliberately unglamorous: a short-lived in-memory JWT, an HttpOnly rotating refresh cookie, single-flight silent refresh, family-based reuse detection, and a same-origin proxy so the whole thing works in every browser's current privacy posture. None of that is novel. All of it is correct.

The UI prioritises clarity over decoration. Validation errors appear as centered red text beneath the exact field that failed. Success appears as a toast. Failures that require action appear as a persistent banner. Icons come from a self-hosted, subset Material Symbols font. There are no hero images, no split-card login screens, and no upload flow for product photos — because none of those help the operator complete a sale faster, and every byte you ship competes with that goal.

Most importantly, the final product will be shaped by what the owner of Thandabantu Spaza Shop confirms is genuinely useful. The queue, expiry and management features are prioritised only according to that feedback.

The objective is not to build the biggest application. It is to build a solution that solves a real problem, works reliably, and can be demonstrated with confidence.
